Version 1, invented data only. Robert, 2026-11-09: in Change selection the Team column is narrow, so names deep down the team tree squeeze and wrap. Add a draggable divider between the Team column and the rest of the screen (where his red line was in the screenshot). Drag it right to widen the Team column; the rest narrows to suit. Drawn for the desktop screen (the screenshot is the desktop Pipeline; Pipeline Mobile has no Change selection).

Two ways to do it, switch between them with the Option buttons below: Option 1 is the draggable divider. Option 2 (Robert's second idea, added the same day) splits Change selection into two tabs: TEAM & PEOPLE (the team tree, the chosen team chip, "Include teams below" and the Owner box) and DATA (everything that is on the right today). In Option 2 the team tab gives the tree the width it needs, and the divider is not used.

  • Where it starts: at its leftmost position, which is today's width (380 px). It cannot go left of that.
  • How far it goes: no more than halfway across the panel. A dashed amber line shows the limit while you drag.
  • How it looks: a slim bar with a grip between the two sides. It turns indigo and thickens when you point at it or drag it. Double-click it to snap back to the start. With the keyboard: Tab to it, then the left and right arrow keys.
  • Try it: (1) open the teams in the Team box (open Mark Mottram, then Ray Vercoe, then Darren Agius); (2) drag the divider right and watch the names un-squeeze and the right side narrow; (3) try to drag past halfway; (4) double-click the divider; (5) switch Light and Dark.
  • Option 2, try it: press 2 Two tabs, then click TEAM & PEOPLE and DATA at the top of the panel to move between the two halves; open the teams in the tree and see how much room the names have.
  • Not shown yet: whether the width is remembered for next time. As drawn it starts at the leftmost position each time you open the screen, as you described. The right side here is simplified (country, campaign, portfolio); the Team tree is the real one.
